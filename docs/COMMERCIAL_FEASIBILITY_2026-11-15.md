# Naija MenuShift — Customer, Channel and Commercial Feasibility Research
**Research date:** 15 November 2026 · **Lead:** Ibrahim · **Status:** Desk research and scenario modelling; **NO customer interviews or measured product economics have been completed by this report.**

## Executive decision
**Keep Challenge 2: AI for Plant-Based Menu Innovation.** Naija MenuShift is a software decision-support tool for adapting Nigerian restaurant menus; **suya is an initial demonstration**, not the entire product. Selling actual tofu suya is a **distinct potential business**. Do not treat selling food and licensing software as the same revenue model.

**Primary paying-customer hypothesis:** independent restaurants, health-conscious eateries, caterers and multi-outlet menu teams. Their problem is repeatable ingredient substitutions, recipe cost and constraints, documented clearly enough to make decisions. **First GTM route:** directly assisted pilots with restaurants/caterers, paid per assessed menu item or subscription **only after** real evidence and willingness-to-pay validation.

**Food pilot route, if the team pursues manufacturing separately:** partnerships with a suya vendor/restaurant to test demand, flavour, serving size, holding time, sourcing and margin. **Supermarket distribution is not the right initial food channel.**

## 1. Evidence about consumer demand and competition
- A multi-country online research project reported that **76%** of surveyed Nigerian respondents said they were highly likely to **try** plant-based meat and **63%** highly likely to **buy** it. These are stated intentions in a survey of younger adults, not observed sales, local suya demand or proven willingness to pay. Nutrition, taste and price were important. Sources: [research fund summary](https://www.fsrfund.org/fsrf-2021-1-012); [methods/case study](https://www.northmountainconsulting.com/case-study-kne-pbm); [Nigeria context report](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5fc0f83868612547ed5e8292/t/67ae23deaafb712b59169588/1739465723005/Nigeria%2Breport%2B2025.pdf).
- Existing food market competition is real. [VeggieVictory's official menu](https://veggievictory.com/menu/), accessed 15 November 2026, lists **Tofu Suya ₦2,500**, **Vchunks Suya ₦2,000**, and **Mushroom Suya ₦5,000** as menu items. Portion weights, actual sales, margins and service charges are not comparable to our pilot, and listed prices can change. This provides an **existence/positioning benchmark**, not proof of demand or profit.
- A 2026 Euromonitor report description says nominal value growth in processed meat and alternatives coexisted with weaker **volume** sales in 2025 because of pressure on household purchasing power. Avoid converting survey interest into revenue projections. [Report abstract](https://www.marketresearch.com/Euromonitor-International-v746/Processed-Meat-Seafood-Alternatives-Nigeria-43693185/).
- An FCT news report described awara as an established soybean-curd food used by some buyers instead of meat; this is a qualitative report, not a current nationwide market survey. [NAN-reported interviews](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2024/02/hardship-fct-consumers-turn-to-awara-as-substitute-for-meat/amp/).

## 2. Customer segments — separate buyers from eaters

| Priority | Buyer / user segment | Pain to validate | Product and sales route | Difficulty/risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Independent restaurants, small health-focused restaurants and chefs | No quick, transparent way to compare menu adaptation cost, nutrition estimates, allergens and feasibility | **Software**: assisted menu assessment, then paid per-dish service or restaurant account | Need credible local pricing and real kitchen performance; buyers may use a spreadsheet instead |
| **2** | Caterers, meal-prep businesses and corporate event food providers | Need plant-based options for groups and dependable ingredient quantities, allergen notices and unit costs | **Software**: pilot a single event-menu adaptation | Event timing and strict food-handling constraints |
| **3** | Suya spots, street-food vendors and kiosks | Need low-cost, consistent skewer preparation and incremental buyers | **Food**: co-tested tofu-suya pilot; **software** only if relevant enough to their operation | Strong price sensitivity, limited cold chain, short preparation window, grilling integrity |
| **4** | Multi-outlet QSRs or restaurant groups | Multiple menus, supplier sensitivity and consistency | **Software**: eventually license for several branches | Longer sales cycles, stronger procurement and compliance demands |
| **Later** | Supermarkets and grocery chains | Want packaged, shelf-stable or chilled products that sell through | **Only a separate packaged food product**, not a primary software customer | NAFDAC requirements, packaging, shelf life, cold chain, returns, listing fees |

**Important distinction:** health-conscious consumers, vegans, flexitarians, students and customers seeking lower-cost protein are **potential diners**, not automatically buyers of the Naija MenuShift software. Restaurants/caterers would pay for software if validated; consumers would buy food from those outlets.

## 3. Channel comparison and recommendations

**Restaurants/caterers — first choice for the software:** direct email/WhatsApp introductions, five-minute demo, one free **research** assessment with clear limitations, then ask for a paid pilot if usefulness is demonstrated. A restaurant requires a repeatable explanation and actionable purchase quantities; this is closest to the intended Challenge 2 use case.

**Suya sellers — first choice for physical-food feedback:** partner with an existing vendor, measure actual quantity, time, texture, serving cost, sales and wastage over a limited supervised trial. Do not assume the vendor will pay a SaaS subscription. Pricing may be too sensitive for costly freeze/thaw methods.

**Supermarkets — defer:** only relevant if a separate packaged tofu/suya product is manufactured with correct safety, packaging, traceability, refrigeration/shelf-life and registration work. [NAFDAC 2025 food registration regulation](https://nafdac.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/Files/Resources/Regulations/NAFDAC-Foods-and-Related-Products-Registration-Regulations-2025.pdf) and [guidance for Nigerian food products](https://www.nafdac.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/Files/Resources/Guidelines/FOOD_GUIDELINES/Guidelines-for-Registration-of-Food-Products-and-Packaged-Water-Made-in-Nigeria.pdf) must be checked with local authorities for the actual product and route. This report does **not** certify legal readiness.

## 5. Tofu versus beef suya — what the data can and cannot show

### Verifiable price *references*
- **Beef (boneless, raw): ₦7,171.41/kg** is the **May 2026 NBS national average**, not today's Zaria/Abuja supplier quote. Source: [NBS selected food price catalogue](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/162/related-materials) and [NBS-derived price table](https://outbid.ng/food-prices).
- **Raw soybeans (exchange spot): ₦614.91/kg** on **13 November 2026**, per [Mansa Markets](https://www.mansamarkets.com/blog/soybean-price-in-nigeria-today). This is not ready-to-cook tofu and **must not** be used as tofu purchase cost without production yield, coagulant, fuel and labour.
- A [Supermart Fresh Tofu listing](https://www.supermart.ng/products/fresh-tofu) shows **₦2,700 per listing** (accessed 15 November), but the listed snippet **does not establish package weight**. Do not convert it to ₦/kg.
- The [VeggieVictory tofu suya menu item](https://veggievictory.com/menu/) lists **₦2,500 sale price**, not ingredient cost. This is a competitor price-positioning benchmark only.

### Transparent scenario, **NOT a real meal-cost quote**
For reproducibility, use the current project's **fictional** purchase masses (which are not measured): plain tofu **140g**, pressed **165g**, frozen/thawed **175g**, pressed/fried **165g**; beef reference **130g of raw beef**. The national beef **raw ingredient-only** cost for 130g at ₦7,171.41/kg is **₦932.28** before seasoning, fuel, losses, packing, labour or cooking yield.

The tofu rows below assume alternative **hypothetical tofu purchase prices**, chosen only to test sensitivity; they are NOT 2026 supplier quotes.

| Case | Hypothetical tofu ₦2,000/kg | ₦4,000/kg | ₦6,000/kg | Maximum tofu price to match ₦932 raw beef ingredient cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Plain tofu, 140g | ₦280 | ₦560 | ₦840 | **₦6,659/kg** |
| Pressed tofu, 165g | ₦330 | ₦660 | ₦990 | **₦5,650/kg** |
| Frozen/thawed tofu, 175g | ₦350 | ₦700 | ₦1,050 | **₦5,327/kg** |
| Pressed/fried tofu, 165g | ₦330 | ₦660 | ₦990 | **₦5,650/kg** |
| Beef, 130g raw | ₦932.28 | ₦932.28 | ₦932.28 | Reference only |

**Interpretation:** At an illustrative ₦4,000/kg purchased tofu, the **main protein ingredient only** appears cheaper than the 130g beef reference at May 2026 NBS pricing. This does **not** mean tofu suya is cheaper per real finished serving, per equivalent protein gram or per retail dish. Pressing/frying and freezing change process costs and final yield. Extra purchased tofu quantities may be required to meet a protein target; source food state and protein per purchase unit must be aligned.

### Correct formula for food economics
`Cost/serving = Σ(purchased ingredient quantity × quoted price per matching unit) + used cooking oil + measured energy/fuel + labour allocation + packaging + attributable wastage/other overheads.`

`Total per-serving gross contribution = actual realized selling price - all attributable variable costs - transaction/delivery fees - spoilage allowance.`

`Break-even tofu purchase price/kg = (beef ingredient cost + beef-specific extra costs - tofu-specific extra costs) / tofu purchased mass (kg)`. The illustrative thresholds in the table assume equal other costs, which is especially unrealistic for the frozen/fried variants.

For the **software**, the economics are different: direct B2B software/service revenue minus customer onboarding, manual research/validation, hosting, support, and acquisition costs. Do not use a tofu plate sale price as software revenue.

### Measurements needed for a valid tofu-versus-beef claim
1. Obtain **three dated local quotes** each for finished tofu/awara, beef, groundnut/yaji, oil, spices and packaging, including exact seller, region, unit and availability.
2. Measure **purchased mass**, finished mass, oil uptake, preparation time and fuel use for **three repeated batches per option** (and one beef reference protocol).
3. Record **total grams of protein per defined finished serving** from compatible source food states, and uncertainty where retention/processing is not measured.
4. Have one food or kitchen expert verify skewering, taste, safety, labelling and practical prep. Allergen cross-contact remains separate.
5. Re-run the software's −20%/base/+20% price stress tests after data review.

## 6. Monetization routes and tests (hypotheses; no customers or revenue confirmed)

### Recommended: software/service first
1. **Assisted restaurant menu assessment:** run a reproducible alternative-protein option, cost, nutrition and sourcing report for one familiar dish; validate whether restaurant owners would pay **per dish**.
2. **Recurring subscription:** once data sources can be maintained, provide repeat comparisons and dated local quote updates. Do not sell recurrent real-price accuracy if the update pipeline is manual/unverified.
3. **Catering/chain package:** multi-menu quotes and prep constraints later; dependent on restaurant evaluation and repeatable workflows.

**Pricing experiments only (not approved prices):** Show independently recruited restaurants three **proposed** prices for a validated per-menu analysis (e.g. ₦5,000 / ₦15,000 / ₦30,000) and ask which they would pay for **today**; corroborate by a paid pilot, not survey responses alone. The company does not have verified willingness-to-pay data, subscriptions or bookings.

### Optional separate food revenue
Partner-operated menu trials may yield plate sales, wholesale prepared protein, or a licensing/supply arrangement. **None of these revenues belong to the software business automatically.** Food safety, traceability, storage, labeling and registration duties need confirmation before sale or distribution.

## 7. Demand, affordability, competition and distribution — go/no-go
- **Demand:** collect at least **8–12 structured B2B interviews** (e.g. 5 restaurant/chef buyers, 2 caterers, 3 suya vendors, 1 specialty-retail/food expert). Target: **three independent merchants agree the problem is important; one agrees to an explicit pilot**. These are **proposed validation gates**, not achieved counts.
- **Affordability:** ask merchants their actual present beef/protein costs, selling price, lost orders, minimum gross margin and how much better the alternative must be; ask for an itemized recent purchase rather than a guess.
- **Competition:** compare the current business process (chef experience, spreadsheets, generic chatbot advice), and existing vegan suya sellers including VeggieVictory. The product wins only if it is more trustworthy, faster or produces measurable margin/decision benefits.
- **Distribution:** software can be delivered by a web link and onboarding; do not assume a vendor has reliable internet/data. Physical tofu distribution adds chilled holding, spoilage, transport, packaging, retail contracts and regulatory obligations.
- **No-go triggers:** tofu cannot hold on skewers; high allergen uncertainty; no equivalent-protein option inside buyer budget; ingredient costs or energy erase margin; no restaurant confirms value; site can't validate data.
